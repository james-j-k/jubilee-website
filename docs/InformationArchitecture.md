# Information Architecture

## Product model

The website is a decision interface, not a digital brochure. Visitors need to establish three things quickly: whether this distributor is credible, whether it serves their need, and what to do next. The architecture therefore progresses from reassurance to relevance to action.

## Primary routes

| Route | Primary visitor question | Business purpose | Primary action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Can I trust this distributor? | Establish quality and guide the next decision. | Explore services / contact |
| About | Who is behind the service? | Humanize the organization and substantiate trust. | Contact us |
| Services | What can I get help with? | Make household services easy to identify. | Request assistance |
| Commercial | Can you support my business? | Qualify commercial demand with a focused proposition. | Start an enquiry |
| Safety | How do I use LPG safely? | Demonstrate duty of care and provide practical value. | Get support |
| Contact | How do I reach you now? | Convert high-intent visitors with minimal friction. | Submit enquiry / call |

## Home-page narrative

1. **Hero — promise, fit, and action.** Communicates the dependable-service promise, offers the primary contact route, and names the household/commercial context. It exists to reduce uncertainty before a visitor invests attention.
2. **Reliability System — make the promise legible.** A concise three-part sequence, such as request, coordinated support, and ongoing help, using only approved operational detail. It exists because process is more credible and distinctive than a row of generic counters.
3. **Evidence module — proof when available.** A verified metric, accreditation, or attributed customer proof is shown only if sourced. It exists because social proof is valuable only when it is specific and believable; otherwise this module disappears.
4. **Service overview — choice architecture.** Presents the few most meaningful household needs rather than a dense catalogue. It exists to help visitors self-select without searching.
5. **Commercial feature — distinct audience branch.** Separates higher-consideration business needs from domestic service. It exists to prevent commercial visitors feeling like an afterthought.
6. **Safety reassurance — care beyond conversion.** A compact gateway to approved safety guidance. It exists to demonstrate responsibility and provide value before a problem becomes urgent.
7. **Closing contact panel — conversion.** Repeats the route to help after the visitor has enough context. It exists because users should never need to return to the top to act.

## Content hierarchy

Each page has one H1 that states the visitor value, a short supporting line, and then chunks content by task. Headings must describe an outcome, not an internal department. For example, “Gas delivery that fits your routine” is clearer than “Our services.”

Safety content is task-oriented: before use, during use, and in an emergency. Commercial content is decision-oriented: suitability, operating support, and how to start. Contact information is always available in the global footer and through the mobile call action, so urgent visitors are never forced through a form. Service-area and availability language appears only once operations approves it; when it is unknown, visitors are invited to confirm fit rather than given a false assurance.

## Content constraints

- Lead with concise, concrete claims that can be substantiated.
- Avoid local-business clichés, stock superlatives, and long founder narratives.
- Use operational proof only where data is confirmed; placeholders must not become factual claims.
- Preserve an editorial rhythm: generous white space, one idea per block, and limited card density.

This structure borrows the clarity of product marketing: it treats the service as a dependable system, while retaining the responsibility and reassurance appropriate to LPG.
