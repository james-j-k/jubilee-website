# UX Principles

## Decision rules

1. **Mobile is the primary product.** Design the shortest, touch-first path before expanding it for larger screens.
2. **Trust is demonstrated.** Prefer approved process, real people, and useful information to claims, counters, or generic badges.
3. **Urgency is respected.** Direct calling and safety guidance remain discoverable; the site never makes a visitor hunt through a decorative experience for help.
4. **Every screen has one job.** A section must orient, explain, prove, qualify, or convert. If it does none, remove it.
5. **Clarity beats cleverness.** Literal labels, concise copy, obvious form feedback, and familiar controls win over novel interactions.
6. **Premium means edited.** Space, typography, image art direction, and precise behavior create quality; more sections and more effects do not.
7. **Speed is part of trust.** A delayed or shifting page feels unreliable, particularly for a service that may be needed urgently.
8. **Accessibility is a design input.** Keyboard, reduced motion, focus visibility, contrast, and plain language are designed from the beginning.

When principles conflict, prioritize safety and task completion, then truthfulness, then accessibility, then visual novelty.
